OPPOSITION TO DEFENDANT JEFFREY SCHATZ’S MOTION TO STRIKE  
 
 Plaintiff/Counterclaim Defendant Substitute Trustees Andrew Brenner, et al (“Trustees”), 
Third-Party Defendants Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (“Wells Fargo”) and custodian, Deutsche Bank 
(“Deutsche Bank”) (collectively “Counterclaim Defendants”), through undersigned counsel, file 
this response in opposition as to Defendant Jeffrey Schatz’s (“Schatz” or “Defendant”) Motion to 
Strike Trustees Motion to Strike or Dismiss the Counterclaim and state as follows:  
Procedural Background  
1. Schatz has filed three separate motions to stay and dismiss the instant foreclosure 
action in which he argues that Trustees lack standing to enforce the subject note  (“Note”).  This 
Court rejected  Schatz’s  arguments  regarding  standing  on  each  occasion  and  denied  each  of  

 
 
2 
 
Schatz’s motions by Orders entered on June 17, 2025, August 21, 2025, and September 16, 2025.  
See Orders (Jun. 17, 2025; Aug. 21, 2025; and Sep. 16, 2025).   
2. Nonetheless,  Schatz  has  continued  to  repeat  the  same unsubstantiated  and  legal  
incorrect standing arguments through his successive applications to the Court relief.  On September 
17, 2025, Schatz filed his Counterclaims, once again asserting that the Trustees lack standing to 
foreclose  and  that  Wells  Fargo  otherwise  lacked  standing  to  appoint  the  Trustees.    See 
Counterclaim  for  Quiet  Title  (Sep.  17,  2025).    Schatz  filed  a  Motion  for  Summary Judgment 
seeking judgment in his favor on his Counterclaims and dismissal of the foreclosure action based 
on the Trustees’ alleged lack of standing.  See Motion for Summary Judgment (Sep. 23, 2025).  
3. The Trustees filed a motion to strike and/or dismiss the counterclaims on October 
3, 2025.  See Motion Strike/Dismiss Counterclaims (Oct. 2, 2025).   
4. On October 7, 2025, Schatz filed a motion seeking to strike the Trustees’ pending 
Motion to Strike and/or Dismiss as well as the Trustees’ responses to his other pending applications 
with the Court.  See Motions to Strike (Oct. 7, 2025; Oct. 9, 2025).   
5. In  his  Motions  to  Strike,  Schatz  alleges  that  the  Trustees  disclosed  confidential  
mediation  communications  in  this  action  by  indicating  that  he  failed  to  exercise  loss  mitigation  
options.  Id.   
6. On October 17, 2025, the Court denied Schatz’s Motion for Summary Judgment, 
thereby  mooting  Schatz’s  motion  to  strike  the  Trustees’  Opposition  to  Schatz’s  Motion  for  
Summary Judgment.  See Order (Oct. 17, 2025).   
7. As a result of Schatz’s voluminous filing history, it is difficult to decipher which of 
Schatz’s numerous motions and filings have been marked deficient or otherwise accepted by the 
Court.   

 
 
3 
 
8. It appears that the only pending motions as to the Counterclaim Defendants that has 
not  been  mooted  is  Schatz’s  Motion  to  Strike  the  Trustee’s  Motion  to  Strike  or  Dismiss  the  
Counterclaim.  
Schatz’s Motions to Strike Should Be Denied 
9. Schatz  argues  in  his  Motion  to  Strike  that the Trustees’ Motion  to  Strike  and/or  
Dismiss his Counterclaims should be stricken because it contains the statement that “[a]fter a failed 
mediation...   Mr.  Schatz  chose  to  reject  a  loan  modification  offer  and  further  loss  mitigation  
options...”.  See Motions to Strike (Oct. 7, 2025; Oct. 9, 2025).     
10. Schatz argues that the Trustees’ response to his affirmative claims directed against 
them constitutes a violation of Maryland Rule 17-109.   
11. Article  17,  Chapter  100  of  the  Maryland  Rules  does  contain  a  Rule  17-109.
1
    It 
appears that Schatz seemingly is referring to Rule 17-105(b)(1) which provides, in relevant part 
that  “a  party  to  a  mediation  and  any  person  or  who  otherwise  participates  in  a  mediation  at  the  
request of a party may not disclose or be compelled to disclose a mediation communication in any 
judicial, administrative, or other proceeding...”  See Md. Rule 17-105(b)(1).   
12. Rule 17-105 does not apply to foreclosure mediation.   Rule 17-101(b)(2) provides 
that “[e]xcept as otherwise provided by Rule, the Rules in this Title do not apply to... an action 
to foreclose a lien against owner-occupied residential property subject to foreclosure mediation 
conducted by the Office of Administrative Hearings under Rule 14-209...”.  See Md. Rule 17-
101(b)(2) (emphasis added).    
 
1
 The former Title 17, constating of sections 17-101 through 17-109 was repealed on November 1, 2012 and replaced 
with the new Title 17 consisting of Sections 17-101 through 17-106 and additional Chapters 2 and 3.  See Md. Rule, 
Title 17, Ch. 100.    

 
 
4 
 
13. The instant proceeding concerns the foreclosure of a lien against owner-occupied 
residential  property and  the  references  to  mediation  concern  statements  made  in  a  Rule  14-209 
proceeding.  Therefore, the statements regarding foreclosure mediation that Schatz cites as a basis 
to strike are not disclosures in violation of Rule 17-105 are not subject to Title 17 of the Maryland 
Rules.   
14. Even if it was subject to limitations of 17-105, Schatz has not established that the 
information  disclosed  in  the  Trustees’  motion  to  strike  or  dismiss  was  confidential  information.    
Nor has Schatz demonstrated that the statements regarding mediation caused him any discernable 
harm or prejudice even if they were confidential.  And, even assuming arguendo, that Schatz could 
make a showing, which he has not and cannot, the any proper remedy would be to remove from 
the public record such offending material from the Trustees’ Motion and not to strike the motion 
in its entirety.    
15. Accordingly, Schatz fails to offer any viable basis to strike the Trustees’ Motion to 
Strike and/or Dismiss the Counterclaims.   
CONCLUSION 
Counterclaim Defendants respectfully request this Court enter an Order denying Schatz’s 
Motions to Strike and granting such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

DEFENDANT'S REPLY IN SUPPORT OF MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT 
Defendant, Jeffrey M. Schatz, in propria persona, submits this Reply in further support of his 
Motion for Summary Judgment. The Plaintiffs' Opposition is a masterclass in misdirection and 
procedural gamesmanship that fails, in every respect, to address the simple, dispositive issue 
before this Court. The Opposition must be disregarded for two primary reasons: (1) it is 
procedurally defective for improperly incorporating by reference a pleading that is the subject of 
a pending Motion to Strike for violating mediation confidentiality ; and (2) it is substantively 
non-responsive, as it argues against a fictitious motion for summary judgment on counterclaims 
that Defendant never filed.    
When these procedural smokescreens are cleared, the undisputed material facts remain, 
established by the Plaintiffs' own binding judicial admissions: the Plaintiffs lacked standing 
when they filed this action, rendering the entire proceeding a legal nullity. Defendant is entitled 
to judgment as a matter of law.

II. THE UNDISPUTED FACTS, ESTABLISHED BY PLAINTIFFS' OWN
ADMISSIONS, MANDATE SUMMARY JUDGMENT. 
7.Once the Plaintiffs' procedural games are set aside, the core issue is simple and
undisputed. Maryland law is clear: a plaintiff in a foreclosure action must have standing at the 
inception of the case. This is a jurisdictional requirement that cannot be cured retroactively. The 
Plaintiffs' own sworn statements prove they failed this test. 
a.The Note Requires Possession: The Promissory Note contains a special
endorsement "WITHOUT RECOURSE PAY TO THE ORDER OF WELLS FARGO BANK, 
N.A.". To be the "holder" entitled to enforce such a note, Wells Fargo must have had physical
possession of the instrument when the action was filed.   
b.Plaintiffs Admit Non-Possession: In their sworn Answer to Interrogatory
No. 1, Plaintiffs made a binding judicial admission that the entity with "current physical 
possession of the original, 'wet-ink' promissory note" is "Custodian: DEUTSCHE BANK... 
SANTA ANA, CA".    
c.The Appointment of Trustees is Void: Because Wells Fargo did not
possess the Note, it was not the "holder" and therefore lacked the authority to execute the 
"Appointment of Substitute Trustees" on August 7, 2024. That appointment is a legal nullity.   
d.The Foreclosure is a Nullity: Because the trustees who filed this action
were never validly appointed, they lacked standing to initiate this foreclosure. The entire action 
was void ab initio.
